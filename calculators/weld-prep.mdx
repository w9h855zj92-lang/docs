The Weld Prep calculator gives you the bevel angle, root gap, and land dimensions for a butt weld joint based on your pipe size and schedule. These values ensure proper joint fit-up for ASME-compliant welds.

## When to use this calculator

Use Weld Prep when preparing pipe ends for butt welding, especially when code-compliant weld profiles are required. Correct bevel geometry is critical for full-penetration welds — too steep a bevel wastes filler metal and time, while too shallow a bevel risks inadequate fusion at the root. Enter your pipe size and schedule and PipeFitter returns the dimensions you need to set up your grinder, machine, or beveling tool.

## Inputs

<ParamField body="Pipe Size (NPS)" type="select" required>
  The nominal pipe size you are preparing for welding. Used to look up the outside diameter and determine the joint geometry.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="Schedule" type="select" required>
  The pipe schedule: **Std**, **XH**, **XXH**, **40**, **80**, **160**, or other standard schedules. The wall thickness — which varies by schedule — determines the root gap and land requirements.
</ParamField>

## Outputs

<ResponseField name="Bevel Angle" type="string">
  The angle to grind or machine on the pipe end. For standard butt welds per ASME B31.3, this is typically **37.5° per side**, producing a 75° included angle (V-groove). PipeFitter will note if your schedule requires a different profile.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="Root Gap" type="string">
  The gap to maintain between the two pipe ends at the root of the weld during fit-up. Measured in inches or millimetres. A consistent root gap ensures complete root penetration.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="Land (Root Face)" type="string">
  The flat portion at the tip of the bevel before the weld groove begins. Also called the root face. A properly sized land prevents burn-through during root pass welding.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="Wall Thickness" type="string">
  The pipe wall thickness looked up from ASME B36.10M for your selected size and schedule, displayed for reference.
</ResponseField>

<Note>
  Weld prep dimensions should always be confirmed against the applicable **Welding Procedure Specification (WPS)** for the job. Code-qualified WPS documents may specify different bevel angles, root gaps, or land dimensions than the ASME defaults — the WPS always takes precedence.
</Note>

<Tip>
  You can look up wall thickness independently using the **Pipe Dimensions** reference table without running a full weld prep calculation. This is useful when you just need to confirm pipe schedule dimensions at the jobsite.
</Tip>
